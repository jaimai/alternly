The **every other weekend custody schedule** is one of the most familiar arrangements in the US. The children live primarily with one parent, and the other parent has them on alternating weekends, often with a midweek visit added in. It's common when parents live farther apart, when work schedules make equal time hard, or when a child simply does better with one home base during the school week.

This guide explains the basic schedule, the most popular midweek variations, and how to make the time that each parent has feel meaningful.

## How the basic schedule works

In the classic version:

- The children live with **Parent A** during the week.
- **Parent B** has them every other weekend, typically from Friday after school to Sunday evening.

Over two weeks, that gives Parent B **2 overnights out of 14**, and Parent A the other 12. It's simple, easy to remember, and requires only a few exchanges a month.

A typical two-week view:

| Day | Week 1 | Week 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Monday–Thursday | Parent A | Parent A |
| Friday | Parent B | Parent A |
| Saturday | Parent B | Parent A |
| Sunday | Parent B (until evening) | Parent A |

## Midweek visit variants

Many families feel twelve days apart is too long for the children to go without seeing one parent. Adding midweek time is the most common fix.

### Every other weekend + a weekly dinner visit

Parent B picks the kids up after school on a set weekday (often Wednesday) and returns them after dinner. No overnight, so the kids still sleep in the same bed every school night, but there's regular contact in the "long" week.

### Every other weekend + a weekly midweek overnight

Parent B has one overnight every week, for example Wednesday after school to Thursday morning at school. Over two weeks, that's roughly **4 overnights out of 14**.

### Extended weekends

Instead of ending on Sunday evening, the weekend runs from **Thursday or Friday after school to Monday morning** drop-off at school. Exchanges happen through school, which can reduce friction, and the visiting parent gets more of the weekend and a school morning.

### Extended weekend + midweek overnight

Combining a Thursday-to-Monday weekend with a weekly overnight can bring Parent B to something like 5 or 6 overnights out of 14. At that point you're getting close to the equal-time schedules like [2-2-5-5](/en/blog/2-2-5-5-custody-schedule).

## Pros of every other weekend

- **Stability for the kids during the school week.** One home base for homework, bedtime and school mornings.
- **Few exchanges.** Great when handoffs are stressful or homes are far apart.
- **Works with long distances or demanding jobs.** Weekend-only time is easier to manage across a longer drive.

## Cons to be honest about

- **Long gaps without the other parent.** Without midweek time, kids can go close to two weeks between visits.
- **The visiting parent can miss everyday life.** Homework, teacher conferences and weeknight activities mostly happen in the other home.
- **"Fun parent" dynamics.** When one parent only sees the kids on weekends, the time can drift toward outings instead of ordinary routines. Both are valuable.

## Making weekend-based time count

If you're the parent with weekend time, a few habits help:

1. **Stay in the loop on school.** Ask for access to the school portal, teacher emails and the activity schedule.
2. **Include routine, not just outings.** Chores, homework check-ins and regular meals make your home feel like home, not a hotel.
3. **Keep a regular call or video time** during the week, if your plan allows.
4. **Show up for activities** when it's appropriate and agreed, even on the other parent's days.

If you're the primary-residence parent, sharing information proactively (report cards, doctor visits, upcoming events) goes a long way toward keeping things calm.

## Holidays and summer on an every-other-weekend plan

Holidays usually override the regular weekend rotation. Many families alternate the major holidays by even and odd years, and give the weekend parent a larger block of summer, since school-year time is limited. Our guides on [splitting holidays](/en/blog/holiday-custody-schedule) and [summer schedules](/en/blog/summer-custody-schedule) cover the common approaches.

Also watch for **three-day weekends**. If your weekend falls on Memorial Day or Labor Day, does the Monday go to the weekend parent? Writing that down ahead of time prevents an argument later.

## Is every other weekend the right fit?

Courts across the US decide custody based on the **best interests of the child**, and state laws differ in how they approach parenting time. Every other weekend is often a starting point rather than a final answer. As children grow, or as parents move closer, many families add midweek time or shift toward a more equal schedule. If you're considering a change, a mediator or family law attorney can explain the options where you live.
